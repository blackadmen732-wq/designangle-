# Free Code Builds Award-Level Animated Websites

Professional studios do not use AI video generators to make award-level sites: they pick one concept, design it in Figma with motion prototyped early, and build it in code with a stack that is now entirely free, namely **GSAP (all plugins free since April 2025), Lenis for smooth scroll, and optionally Three.js for one WebGL canvas** ([GSAP 3.13 release](https://gsap.com/blog/3-13/), [Lenis](https://github.com/darkroomengineering/lenis)). The "expensive" look comes from a short list of repeatable mechanics (masked text reveals, a pinned section scrubbed by scroll, image sequences drawn to canvas, one consistent easing curve) applied as a single system with one tempo, on top of disciplined typography and real, colour-graded imagery. Premium visuals cost close to nothing when they come from CC0 3D libraries, Blender renders scrubbed on scroll, free-licence stock given one uniform treatment, and code-generated shaders and grain. Claude Code reaches this quality when it is constrained and checked: one design skill, the official GSAP skills, a DESIGN.md file, real product facts in the brief, and rendered checks at phone and desktop widths. Awwwards jurors score **Design 40%, Usability 30%, Creativity 20%, Content 10%**, so most ambitious sites lose on frame rate and mobile, not on missing effects ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/about-evaluation/)). On price, animation is not what clients pay $10,000 for: in the one dedicated freelancer survey found, **only about 2% of projects exceed $10K**, and the designers who get there sell strategy, brand, content and integrations around the build ([Web Designer Academy](https://webdesigneracademy.com/state-of-web-designer-pricing-2025/)). For a restaurant, a $10K+ fee is credible only when it bundles things like brand identity, a photo shoot, an editable HTML menu, embedded reservations and multi-location SEO; a five-page animated brochure sits in the $1,500 to $4,000 tier by agencies' own definitions ([Blue Ridge](https://weareblueridge.com/how-much-does-a-restaurant-website-cost/)).

## Studios pick one idea first, then prototype motion before any code

The studio write-ups that could be read (Studio Freight, Resn, Unseen, Balky Studio, and Awwwards-jury freelancers) describe the same order of work. It starts with listening and strategy, moves to references and a moodboard, lands on one concept, fixes type and colour, tests layouts in real pages, prototypes motion, and only then builds. Designer Abhishek Jha, whose TrueKind site won Awwwards Site of the Day, puts it plainly: "Every project I work on begins with listening. Before touching any design tools, I immersed myself in the client's vision, mood, and tone." He budgeted **one to two months for design alone** and threw away the first version ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/06/25/designing-truekind-a-skincare-brands-journey-through-moodboards-motion-and-meaning/)). No source describes skipping straight to high-fidelity screens.

References become art direction, not a copy, through three habits. First, curate by mood, not prettiness: pick "pictures that match the brand's energy, saturation, color language, and atmosphere", then adjust exposure, warmth and contrast until the board looks like one world ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/06/25/designing-truekind-a-skincare-brands-journey-through-moodboards-motion-and-meaning/)). Second, look outside web design: Studio Freight built the Psyop identity from the title sequences of Scorsese and Tarantino films, and a browser launch from orbital paths ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/07/29/studio-freight-moving-missions-forward/)). Third, reduce to one memorable device. Balky Studio's rule for the 1820 Productions site was "Remove anything unnecessary. Keep only what could serve as a strong identity for the project and then amplify it across all pages", which produced a near-monochrome site with "very few font sizes" ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/02/13/1820-productions-minimal-design-maximal-motion/)). Unseen Studio does the same thing by replacing a category's default format with one bespoke device, such as a 3D project ring in place of a scrolling gallery ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/07/20/the-craft-behind-memorable-digital-experiences-inside-unseen-studio/)). Which inspiration galleries professionals use day to day could not be verified from primary sources, so this report names none.

Motion is designed as video before development. Balky made "early tests in Figma and more complex sequences were moved to Jitter", and found that "showing motion prototypes helped us explain ideas faster than static frames" ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/02/13/1820-productions-minimal-design-maximal-motion/)). The motion vocabulary is also named at this stage: line animations as section markers, parallax on featured images, and transitions that "feel closer to editorial cuts than standard fades", all kept at "the same tempo and logic". One award-oriented studio's published five-week plan shows how the phases overlap: week 1 art direction (references, typography, palette, signature moment), weeks 2 to 3 all pages with key animations tested, weeks 3 to 4 development, week 5 polish and real-device testing ([Ecrin Digital](https://www.ecrin.digital/newsroom/awwwards-level-website-criteria-budget-timeline)). That schedule comes from a studio selling the offer, so read it as a best case. The smallest documented award-winning team is two people, a designer and a creative developer.

The steps below turn that process into something one person can run.

| Step | What to do | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Interview the client; audit what competitors' sites look like and decide where to go the other way | One-paragraph positioning |
| 2 | Build a moodboard from film, print, architecture and a few sites; grade the images to match | One coherent board |
| 3 | Write one concept sentence and one signature moment | The "one memorable thing" |
| 4 | Choose one display typeface and one text typeface, 4 to 6 colours, a short size scale | Design tokens |
| 5 | Design the hero and one inner page in Figma at desktop and phone widths, with real copy and images | Two tested layouts |
| 6 | Prototype the hero reveal and one scroll transition as a short clip in Figma or Jitter | Motion reference video |
| 7 | Write the tokens and motion rules into a DESIGN.md | Brief for the coding agent |
| 8 | Build the hero alone in code, review, then the rest | Working site |
| 9 | Test on a real mid-range phone; add reduced-motion fallbacks | Shippable site |

## Six mechanics produce nearly every signature effect

The whole GSAP toolset, including the formerly paid SplitText, ScrollSmoother, MorphSVG, DrawSVG and Flip plugins, has been **free for commercial use since version 3.13 on 29 April 2025**, funded by Webflow ([GSAP 3.13 release](https://gsap.com/blog/3-13/), [Webflow](https://webflow.com/updates/gsap-becomes-free)). It is free under GreenSock's own licence, not an open-source one, which matters only to teams with open-source-only rules ([GSAP on GitHub](https://github.com/greensock/GSAP)). Lenis is the default smooth-scroll library; Locomotive Scroll version 5 is itself now built on top of it ([Locomotive Scroll](https://github.com/locomotivemtl/locomotive-scroll)). Motion, the renamed Framer Motion, is the usual choice for React interface transitions ([Motion](https://github.com/motiondivision/motion)). Native CSS now handles the simple cases: scroll-driven animations work across the latest major browsers as of October 2026, and same-document view transitions have been Baseline since October 2025, though Firefox still lacks cross-document view transitions ([caniuse](https://caniuse.com/wf-scroll-driven-animations), [web.dev](https://web.dev/blog/same-document-view-transitions-are-now-baseline-newly-available), [caniuse](https://caniuse.com/cross-document-view-transitions)).

**Masked text reveals.** Split a heading into lines with `SplitText.create(heading, { type: "lines, words, chars", mask: "lines" })`, then animate each line from `yPercent: 110` to `0`. The published reference values are lines at 0.8 seconds with a 0.08 second stagger, words at 0.6 with 0.06, letters at 0.4 with 0.008, on the curve `cubic-bezier(0.625, 0.05, 0, 1)`. Run it inside `document.fonts.ready` so line breaks are measured with the final font ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/05/14/from-splittext-to-morphsvg-5-creative-demos-using-free-gsap-plugins/)).

**Pin and scrub.** ScrollTrigger pins a section and ties one animation to scroll progress. `scrub: true` locks it directly to the scrollbar; a number such as `scrub: 0.5` adds a half-second catch-up, which is the smoothing professionals use. Two documented mistakes to avoid: do not animate the pinned element itself (animate its children), and do not nest ScrollTriggers inside a timeline's child tweens ([ScrollTrigger docs](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/Plugins/ScrollTrigger/)).

**Horizontal scroll.** "The trick is that the page still scrolls vertically. You pin a viewport-sized section, then bind a horizontal tween to that pin's scroll range" ([Annnimate](https://annnimate.com/learn/scroll/horizontal-scroll)).

**Apple-style image sequences.** Export numbered frames, draw them to a `<canvas>`, pin the section and let ScrollTrigger scrub a single frame number. A working configuration is three viewport heights of scroll on desktop, two on phones, and `scrub: 0.5`; under reduced motion show one still with no pin and no bulk download ([GSAP Vault](https://gsapvault.com/blog/scroll-image-sequence-tutorial)).

**Pointer effects.** A magnetic button feeds the pointer offset from the element's centre into `gsap.quickTo(el, "x", { duration: 1, ease: "elastic.out(1, 0.3)" })` and resets to zero on mouse leave ([Olivier Larose](https://blog.olivierlarose.com/tutorials/magnetic-button)).

**Layout and page transitions.** GSAP Flip records a layout, lets you change it, then animates the difference: `Flip.getState(targets)`, change the DOM, `Flip.from(state, …)` ([GSAP Flip docs](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/Plugins/Flip/)). ToyFight's Flim site builds its page transitions from two GSAP timelines animating a CSS mask variable, with no WebGL at all ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/01/14/a-site-for-sore-eyes-combining-gsap-and-webflow-to-showcase-flim/)).

One wiring rule prevents the most common bug. Run everything from a single frame loop. The official Lenis and ScrollTrigger hookup is `lenis.on('scroll', ScrollTrigger.update); gsap.ticker.add((time) => { lenis.raf(time * 1000); }); gsap.ticker.lagSmoothing(0);` ([Lenis](https://github.com/darkroomengineering/lenis)). A 2026 case study traced WebGL lagging exactly one frame behind the page to Lenis and React Three Fiber each running their own loop ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/08/15/inside-haoqi-design-letting-dom-and-webgl-share-a-retro-futurist-stage/)). In React, wrap animations in `useGSAP()` from `@gsap/react` so they clean up on unmount ([GSAP React guide](https://gsap.com/resources/React/)). Use `gsap.matchMedia()` to give phones and reduced-motion users a different, lighter version ([gsap.matchMedia docs](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/GSAP/gsap.matchMedia%28%29/)).

WebGL is optional. When studios use it, the core craft is not 3D modelling but keeping real `<img>` elements in the page and mirroring each with a shader-driven plane on one fixed canvas, positioned from `getBoundingClientRect()` ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/02/02/building-a-scroll-revealed-webgl-gallery-with-gsap-three-js-astro-and-barba-js/)). Two respected 2026 studio sites, Obys and Lesse, used no GSAP and little or no WebGL, which shows the stack is not what separates good from great ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/05/14/designing-ourselves-the-new-obys-identity-and-website/), [Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/06/05/the-making-of-the-new-lesse-studio-website-clarity-performance-and-intentionality/)).

## Premium visuals cost almost nothing without AI video

The cheapest route to "premium 3D" is often no real-time 3D at all. Render an animation in Blender, export frames, and scrub them on scroll. Lighting and materials are free and commercially clean: Poly Haven states "Our assets are all licensed as CC0", and ambientCG, Quaternius and Kenney use the same public-domain licence with no credit required ([Poly Haven](https://polyhaven.com/license), [ambientCG](https://docs.ambientcg.com/license/), [Quaternius](https://quaternius.com/faq.html), [Kenney](https://kenney.nl/support)). A per-frame budget from one low-authority source is 15 to 40 KB for a 1200 by 800 WebP at quality 75, so 90 frames lands near 2.25 MB ([skills.sh](https://www.skills.sh/avnehsbhatia/ultraui/image-sequence-scrubbing)). Apple's AirPods Pro page used 148 frames and serves a single fallback image on lighter connections ([CSS-Tricks](https://css-tricks.com/lets-make-one-of-those-fancy-scrolling-animations-used-on-apple-product-pages/)).

If you prefer one video file to many images, the video must be encoded with every frame as a keyframe, or scrubbing stutters. The command is `ffmpeg -i in.mov -vf scale=1280:-2 -r 30 -movflags faststart -vcodec libx264 -crf 23 -g 1 -pix_fmt yuv420p -an out.mp4`, where `-g 1` is the part that matters. Expect roughly 8 MB at 1280 pixels wide against 1 to 2 MB for a normal encode ([Hon Tran](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/scroll-scrubbed-video-stutters-fix)). If you do load a real 3D model, compress it in one pass with `gltf-transform optimize input.glb output.glb --compress draco --texture-compress webp` ([gltf-transform](https://gltf-transform.dev/cli)). Spline's free tier is a prototyping tool only: exports carry a watermark and there is no code or video export ([Spline pricing](https://spline.design/pricing)).

For photos and video, Unsplash, Pexels and Coverr all allow commercial use without attribution ([Unsplash](https://unsplash.com/license), [Pexels](https://www.pexels.com/license/), [Coverr](https://coverr.co/license)). Mixkit needs a per-clip check because it has separate "Free" and "Restricted" video licences ([Mixkit](https://mixkit.co/license/)). None of these licences clears logos, artworks or a person's likeness, so hero images without recognisable people or brands are the low-risk choice. The Met's Open Access programme adds more than 492,000 public-domain images that few competitor sites use ([The Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/hubs/open-access)).

Stock stops looking like stock when one treatment is applied to every image. Duotones "help unify a wide range of photography by applying a consistent color treatment" ([University of Oregon](https://communications.uoregon.edu/duotones)), and "applying the same crops and adjustments across images keeps your brand visually unified" ([Adobe Express](https://www.adobe.com/express/learn/blog/brand-consistent-photography-tips-for-small-businesses)). Film grain is a few lines of SVG: an `feTurbulence` filter with `type='fractalNoise' baseFrequency='0.65' numOctaves='3'` layered under a gradient ([CSS-Tricks](https://css-tricks.com/grainy-gradients/)). Because the grade, grain and mask live in CSS, every future image inherits the look.

Animated backgrounds are free too, but licences vary sharply.

| Source | Cost | Safe for a paying client? |
|---|---|---|
| Your own CSS gradients and SVG grain | Free | Yes |
| Paper Shaders | Free, Apache 2.0 | Yes ([repo](https://github.com/paper-design/shaders)) |
| shaders.com free content | Free, engine MIT | Yes; Pro collections need a plan ([licence](https://shaders.com/license)) |
| Unicorn Studio | Free tier has logo; commercial licence at $168/year | Only on the paid plan ([pricing](https://www.unicorn.studio/pricing)) |
| Shadertoy code | Free to view | No by default: non-commercial licence ([terms](https://www.shadertoy.com/terms)) |

Fonts carry the biggest hidden trap. Fontshare fonts are "100% free for personal and commercial use", but its closed-source licence forbids subsetting, format conversion and handing the files to a client, who must download their own copy ([Fontshare](https://www.fontshare.com/licenses/itf-ffl)). Pangram Pangram, whose PP Mori and Editorial Neue appear on award sites, is free for personal use only; commercial licences start at $40 and both designer and client need one ([Pangram Pangram FAQ](https://pangrampangram.com/pages/faq), [Pangram Pangram](https://pangrampangram.com/)). Open-licence foundries such as Collletttivo are the least restrictive ([Collletttivo](https://www.collletttivo.it/licensing)). For icons, Lucide, Phosphor, Tabler and Heroicons are permissively licensed ([allsvgicons](https://allsvgicons.com/blog/best-free-svg-icon-libraries-2026/)).

AI still images have an honest, narrow place. Nielsen Norman Group found unlabelled AI images did not reduce trust, but when viewers suspected an image was AI they rated the site lower ([NN/g](https://nngroup.com/articles/ai-generated-images)). Use them for textures and atmosphere that will be graded anyway, and use real photographs for anything a visitor reads as evidence: the product, the team, the room. A half-day local shoot is quoted at roughly $500 to $1,500 in one 2026 guide and up to $3,000 in another; these are vendor figures, not survey data ([ProShoot](https://proshoot.io/blog/how-much-does-brand-photography-cost-2026/), [BizToolkit](https://www.biztoolkit.co/post/brand-photographer-rates-in-2026-per-session-package-pricing)).

## A professional look reduces to rules you can check

The well-regarded references agree on a small set of measurable rules. They matter more than any effect.

| Area | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Body text | 15 to 25 px, line spacing 120 to 145%, 45 to 90 characters per line | [Practical Typography](https://practicaltypography.com/summary-of-key-rules.html) |
| Typefaces | One or two families, chosen deliberately; very few sizes | [Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/02/13/1820-productions-minimal-design-maximal-motion/) |
| Hierarchy | Use weight and colour before size; two weights; two or three text tones, dark but not pure black | [Refactoring UI](https://medium.com/refactoring-ui/7-practical-tips-for-cheating-at-design-40c736799886) |
| Spacing | Multiples of 8, with a 4-point baseline grid | [Spec.fm](https://spec.fm/specifics/8-pt-grid) |
| Contrast | Body text at least 4.5:1 | [Paralect](https://www.paralect.com/blog/signs-of-ai-slop-in-design) |
| Grouping | Separate with space or background before borders | [Refactoring UI](https://medium.com/refactoring-ui/7-practical-tips-for-cheating-at-design-40c736799886) |
| Depth | Two-layer shadows; inner radius no larger than outer | [Vercel](https://vercel.com/design/guidelines) |
| Touch | Tap targets 44 px on mobile; inputs at least 16 px | [Vercel](https://vercel.com/design/guidelines) |
| Images | Real or commissioned, graded to one atmosphere; explicit dimensions | [Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/06/25/designing-truekind-a-skincare-brands-journey-through-moodboards-motion-and-meaning/), [Vercel](https://vercel.com/design/guidelines) |

Motion has two regimes, and mixing them up is a common error. Interface feedback (buttons, menus, tooltips) should be fast: "UI animations should generally stay under 300ms", using ease-out, never starting from `scale(0)` ([Emil Kowalski](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/7-practical-animation-tips)). Editorial motion on a marketing site (hero reveals, scroll scenes) runs longer, in the 0.4 to 0.8 second range per unit with small staggers, as in the text-reveal values above. Exponential ease-out is the house curve of the genre; it is the default in both Lenis and ScrollSmoother ([Lenis](https://github.com/darkroomengineering/lenis), [ScrollSmoother docs](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/Plugins/ScrollSmoother/)). In both regimes, animate only `transform` and `opacity` ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/animations-guide)) and honour `prefers-reduced-motion` ([Vercel](https://vercel.com/design/guidelines)). No source gave verified durations for award-site page transitions, so tune those by eye.

What makes a site look cheap or AI-generated in 2026 is a recognisable set of defaults: the Inter typeface, an indigo-to-purple gradient, three rounded cards in a row and a generic centred hero. As one agency put it, "None of these choices are bad on their own. The problem is that they are not choices at all" ([925Studios](https://www.925studios.co/blog/ai-slop-design-tells)). Paralect's list adds all-caps eyebrow labels above every heading, emoji as icons, glass cards over colour blobs, the same radius and shadow on everything, and a fade-up on every block; its fix is "Make 1 thing on the page memorable and keep the rest simple" ([Paralect](https://www.paralect.com/blog/signs-of-ai-slop-in-design)). These are agency opinions, not measured studies. The list also moves: Anthropic's own design skill now names warm cream with a serif and terracotta accent, and near-black with one acid accent, as the new clusters to avoid ([Anthropic SKILL.md](https://github.com/anthropics/skills/blob/main/skills/frontend-design/SKILL.md)). The durable lesson is that studio motion differs from generic motion by system, not quantity: a small named vocabulary at one tempo, against an identical fade on every block.

## Claude Code reaches this level when constrained, then checked

Anthropic's explanation for generic output is that without direction a model samples the "high-probability center" of web design, and that it is highly steerable when told what to do in terms a front-end engineer would implement ([Anthropic](https://claude.com/blog/improving-frontend-design-through-skills)). The tooling below exists to supply that direction and to verify the result. Star counts were read on 22 November 2026 and measure attention, not quality.

|---|---|---|
| Design direction (pick one) | [Anthropic frontend-design](https://github.com/anthropics/skills/blob/main/skills/frontend-design/SKILL.md) | Install with `/plugin install frontend-design@claude-plugins-official`; plans tokens before building |
| Design direction plus audit | [Impeccable](https://github.com/pbakaus/impeccable) | `npx impeccable install`; 24 commands and a 62-rule deterministic detector |

| Design source | [Figma MCP](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/32132100833559-Guide-to-the-Figma-MCP-server) | Reads frames and variables; needs a Figma seat |

The evidence that design skills help is thin and should be stated honestly. The best independent test ran one brief through four skills and found "The skills change direction more than quality", at 1.6 to 7.6 times the cost of no skill; Anthropic's own short skill gave the best cost-to-benefit, and only Impeccable stopped the model inventing product facts. The author notes "One run per skill means this is an example, not a benchmark", and sells a competing product ([SkillGild](https://skillgild.dev/learn/claude-code-frontend-design-skills)). The same test advises one direction skill at a time, because two "tend to argue".

One caution is specific to animated sites. Anthropic's current skill tells the model to "use non-user-triggered motion sparingly" and treats fade-ups on every section as an AI tell, while adding that "the brief's own words always win" ([Anthropic SKILL.md](https://github.com/anthropics/skills/blob/main/skills/frontend-design/SKILL.md)). If you want an Awwwards-style scroll experience, the brief must say so and name the motion vocabulary.

The workflow with the most support is constrain, then verify. Write a brief that contains real product facts and forbids inventing others, the audience, the one action you want, the fonts and colours to keep, and the looks to avoid. Ask for the plan (palette, type, layout) before any code, and ask for checks at 375 and 1440 pixels ([SkillGild](https://skillgild.dev/learn/claude-code-frontend-design-skills)). Keep the design decisions in a DESIGN.md file, a Google Labs format that pairs exact token values with the reasoning behind them ([design.md](https://github.com/google-labs-code/design.md)). Build one section at a time: "a single section reviewed closely beats a whole page at a glance" ([Impeccable](https://github.com/pbakaus/impeccable)). The component libraries are parts to restyle, not a design; their beams, marquees and bento grids are widely recognised. One real limit remains: these tools capture still screenshots, so none of them lets the agent judge motion timing. You watch the animation and give the correction. An agency that reversed its Webflow recommendation in 2026 put the trade-off this way: "Claude will happily build something mediocre if you let it. The teams getting standout results give it a real design system and review like editors, not spectators" ([Conversion Factory](https://conversionfactory.co/blog/best-website-builder-for-saas-marketing-sites-webflow-vs-framer-vs-custom-code)).

## Award case studies reward pipelines and tuning, not more effects

Twelve builder-written breakdowns from 2023 to 2026 were read. They are detailed on technique and almost silent on budget, team size and weeks, so no typical figures can be stated. The repeated core is a framework for content plus a CMS, GSAP and Lenis for page motion, and Three.js on a single shared canvas. ToyFight's INK Games build explains the canvas choice: "having multiple canvases can really kill performance and some browsers limit how many 3D contexts you can have at the same time" ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/11/21/one-canvas-to-rule-them-all-how-ink-games-new-site-handles-complex-3d/)).

Four tactics recur. Sites show something instantly and swap in the heavy version: basement.studio's Vercel Ship hero animates as SVG first, because "waiting for the hero shader to be ready wasn't an option" ([basement.studio](https://basement.studio/ai/post/shipping-ship-behind-the-particle-shader-effect-for-vercels-conf)). They fake 3D where possible: INK Games' portals are plain CSS 3D rotation, and Flim syncs page elements to a physics engine with no renderer at all. They bake lighting into textures instead of computing it live ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/unseen-studio-by-unseen-studio-wins-sotm-february-2023.html)). And they treat mobile as a tiered downgrade of the same site: Bruno Simon's portfolio "automatically switches to a lower quality preset" on phones ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/brunos-portfolio-case-study.html)). Shopify's team summarised the top tier as "high-end browser work is mostly pipeline work" ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/06/26/engineering-the-web-experience-behind-shopifys-spring-26-edition-everywhere/)).

Time goes into tuning. Shader.se's developers wrote that "A transition can be technically correct for days before it actually feels good" ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/05/19/80s-business-tech-seamless-scene-transitions-inside-shader-ses-scroll-driven-webgpu-pipeline/)), and Obys that small changes in timing and spacing "took the longest to resolve" ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/05/14/designing-ourselves-the-new-obys-identity-and-website/)). Studio portfolio sites are a misleading benchmark: Unseen's rebrand ran over four years with one near-finished build scrapped, and Bruno Simon's took a little over a year. That polish is not what a client budget normally buys.

The judging confirms where the points are. Juror Hon Tran notes "Design and Usability together are 70% of your score. Creativity — the flashy WebGL, the clever scroll — is only 20%", and that "A hero animation that dips below 60fps costs more Usability than the animation earns in Creativity". He also names the discounted tropes: "a cursor-follow blob, a default Lenis scroll, a boilerplate WebGL plane" ([Hon Tran](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/awwwards-judging-criteria)). A second juror says usability "is where most ambitious sites lose their award", with common losses from neglecting mobile and "polishing the homepage and rushing the rest" ([Ecrin Digital](https://www.ecrin.digital/newsroom/awwwards-level-website-criteria-budget-timeline)). Both jurors sell site builds, and heavy sites do still win: a third-party measurement put the 2024 Site of the Year at 15.4 MB ([DappaSol](https://dappasol.com/guides/immersive-website-examples/)). The penalty is for jank and slow first paint, not file size as such. One designer's caveat belongs here too: TrueKind's client later "shifted toward simpler, more familiar designs" because "Creative websites are a feast for the eyes, but they don't always convert perfectly" ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/06/25/designing-truekind-a-skincare-brands-journey-through-moodboards-motion-and-meaning/)).

## The $10K fee buys strategy, content and integrations

The pricing data is weak but points one way. In a 208-response survey of freelancers and small agency owners, the median project fell in the $2,500 to $5,000 band and **about 2% exceeded $10,000**; a chart on the same page suggests up to 6%, so treat 2 to 6% as the range. The sample is self-selected from the publisher's audience. Within it, the 15% who charge for strategy up front "are more than 2× more likely to land $5k+ projects", and the top earners most often sell brand strategy alongside design ([Web Designer Academy](https://webdesigneracademy.com/state-of-web-designer-pricing-2025/)). Clutch reports the same shape for agencies: "Most web design projects reviewed on Clutch usually cost less than $10,000", with a $38,105 average pulled up by a minority of large projects ([Clutch](https://clutch.co/web-designers/pricing)). One award-oriented studio publishes €6,000 for a focused marketing site in 4 to 8 weeks and €12,000 for an immersive one with 3D in 8 to 12 weeks ([Ecrin Digital](https://www.ecrin.digital/newsroom/awwwards-level-website-criteria-budget-timeline)).

No source gave a verified checklist of what a $10K+ engagement contains, and none of the pricing sources names animation as a price driver. The reading that fits the evidence is that $500 to $3,000 buys a build, and $10,000 buys a process around the build: paid discovery, brand and positioning, a custom design system, content production, integrations and ongoing care. Motion craft is how the work looks worth the fee; it is not the reason for the fee.

For a restaurant, a hospitality agency's published tiers make this concrete. Every restaurant pricing source found is a vendor, so treat the numbers as indicative.

| Tier | Price | What is delivered |
|---|---|---|
| Template | $1,500 to $4,000 | Menu, hours, location, a reservation link |
| Custom | $4,000 to $10,000 | Custom design, photography integration, an easy-to-update menu system, ordering or reservation flow, SEO foundation |
| Premium | $10,000+ | Multi-location architecture, gift card or merchandise store, event booking, custom integration with OpenTable or Resy, or a full brand identity plus website |

Source: [Blue Ridge](https://weareblueridge.com/how-much-does-a-restaurant-website-cost/).

The fundamentals decide a restaurant site before any animation does. The menu must be real web text, not a PDF. The booking or ordering action sits above the fold and stays sticky on mobile. Photography shows the actual dishes and room. The page is usable "in under three seconds on cellular" ([Suff Digital](https://www.suffdigital.com/resources/guides/best-restaurant-websites)). The menu page should carry `Restaurant` schema with `hasMenu`, and the name, address and hours should match the Google Business Profile ([Beyond Menu](https://get.beyondmenu.com/blog/restaurant-menu-seo)); the same guide cites research that 84.7% of restaurant sites use no restaurant schema, which makes it an easy, visible win. For fine dining, "Atmosphere lives around the booking flow, not in front of it" ([Suff Digital](https://www.suffdigital.com/resources/guides/best-restaurant-websites)). The agency's own warning is the right note to design by: "A $3,000 template site that loads fast, looks clean, and gets out of the guest's way will outperform a $15,000 overbuilt site that's slow and hard to navigate" ([Blue Ridge](https://weareblueridge.com/how-much-does-a-restaurant-website-cost/)). The client also pays the reservation platform separately; OpenTable's US plans run $149 to $499 a month plus per-cover fees according to a third-party roundup ([Restaurant Snapshot](https://restaurantsnapshotforghl.com/blog/opentable-vs-resy-vs-tock-vs-sevenrooms-cost/)).

## Conclusion

The research inverts the usual assumption about where the money and the difficulty are. The animation stack is free and its recipes are public down to the easing curve, so effects are no longer a moat. What stays scarce is the part before the code (one specific idea, a chosen typeface, real graded imagery) and the part after it (days of tuning timing, and holding 60 frames per second on a mid-range phone). Paid AI video tools address neither, which is why they felt expensive for what they returned.

For an app designer this is good news, because the scarce parts are design judgment, not engineering. A practical first project is deliberately small: one concept, two typefaces, one signature scroll moment built from a Blender or stock sequence, masked text reveals at one tempo, and a reduced-motion fallback, built section by section with Claude Code under a DESIGN.md. To charge $10,000 for it, the proposal has to lead with what the client recognises as business value (positioning, photography, copy, booking or ordering that works, search visibility, a maintenance plan) and let the motion be the proof of care. Two things remain unknown from this research: what large studios actually charge and staff, since none disclose it, and whether any design skill reliably improves agent output, since every test so far is a single run.
