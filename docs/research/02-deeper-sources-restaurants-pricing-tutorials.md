# Award Restaurant Sites Run On Modest Stacks

The award-recognised restaurant sites of 2024 to 2026 are simpler than they look: most run on **Webflow or WordPress with GSAP, Lenis smooth scroll and Swiper**, use one display typeface over a plain sans, a two-colour palette of a deep ground and a warm light, put the menu in real HTML or a per-venue PDF, and hand booking to a platform such as Zenchef or OpenTable. None builds its own reservations ([Ballena](https://ballenacabo.com/), [Los Tontos](https://lostontos.be/), [La table de Joakim](https://latabledejoakim.fr/)). The awards go to the edges of the site (loader, page transition, hover on a list, footer, 404 page) at least as much as to the hero, and WebGL appears on only 4 of the 15 sites checked. The recipes for those effects are public down to the number: two easing curves, **`cubic-bezier(0.76, 0, 0.24, 1)` for full-screen moves and `cubic-bezier(0.33, 1, 0.68, 1)` for text reveals**, cover most of what you need ([Olivier Larose](https://blog.olivierlarose.com/tutorials/awwwards-landing-page), [easings.net source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ai/easings.net/master/src/easings.yml)). Studios that win awards describe small cross-skilled teams with no design-to-developer handoff, and they list minimums of **$30,000 to $100,000**; the one named top freelancer with a public price starts at $10,000 ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/lusion/), [Webflow](https://webflow.com/@dennissnellenberg)). Among 20 small studios that publish prices the median starting price is **$12,000**, but those buyers are software companies; every publicly priced restaurant offer found sits under $1,000 or at $99 to $499 a month ([DIXI](https://www.dixi.agency/blog/saas-web-design-agencies-pricing), [Popmenu](https://get.popmenu.com/pricing)). A custom restaurant stack (Sanity plus Vercel) costs about $20 to $85 a month to run, and the reservation platform adds $149 to $499 a month that the restaurant pays whoever builds the site ([OpenTable](https://www.opentable.com/restaurant-solutions/plans/)). Three limits apply throughout: the site teardowns come from page source and text, not from watching the motion; almost every price is vendor-published; and no first-hand pricing account from a solo freelancer or testimony from a restaurant owner was found.

## Award restaurant sites pair two colours with a borrowed booking engine

Fifteen sites with award pages were checked. Only two are trading restaurants with an Awwwards Site of the Day: **Tripletta Pizza (4 April 2025) and Amici (2 October 2024)**. The other winners in the category are safari lodges, an Antarctic camp operator, a coffee brand and a burger concept; real single restaurants mostly sit at Honorable Mention ([Awwwards Hotel / Restaurant](https://www.awwwards.com/websites/hotel-restaurant/)). That tier is the realistic reference set for a restaurant brief. Site of the Day scores in this category cluster around 7.2 to 7.4 out of 10, and usability is usually the lowest of the four jury marks (Tripletta 6.98), so even winners pay a usability price for their motion ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/tripletta-pizza)).

Four patterns come out of this set. The first is **type**: one characterful display face plus a neutral text face. Fine dining and luxury use a serif (Instrument Serif at Joakim, PP Fragment at the [Tengile](https://tengilemalamala.com/) lodges); modern French restaurants use a geometric sans (Futura PT, Gotham); casual brands use condensed or cartoon faces (Oswald, Modak). The second is **colour**: a deep ground with a warm light, such as olive and cream or terracotta and sand, with casual brands adding accents on top. Pure black and white appears once, and even there the stylesheet carries browns and creams. The third is **photography**: casual sites lead with people and place (Tripletta's image descriptions are a delivery rider and a skater, not pizza), fine dining leads with one plate or one landscape (Ballena opens on a drone aerial), and no homepage opens on a grid of dishes ([Tripletta](https://www.triplettapizza.com/), [Ballena](https://ballenacabo.com/)). The fourth is **the hero as a choice**: Tripletta's hero is four photo tiles for deliver, take away, eat in and shop, and Los Tontos' hero is two buttons, reserve and view menu ([Los Tontos](https://lostontos.be/)).

The motion kit is equally repeatable. The string `lenis` is in the source of 7 of the 12 sites fetched, Swiper in four, and Barba page transitions in three. Awwwards records a preloader on five sites and a page transition on three; Crav's loader uses copy as the joke ("ADDING CRISPY GARDEN LETTUCE..."). Repeating text bands appear at Tripletta (city names, "miam!") and Amici (a list of occasions). The details that Awwwards singled out are small and cheap: Amici's "Home - Trail Effect", "Hover listing restaurant", "404 page - Stickers" and "Footer - Loyalty card with logo" ([Amici award page](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/amici)). Video openers belong to the travel sites, not the restaurants. A reserve action stays in reach everywhere: in the header at Ballena, in the top bar at Tengile, five times down the page at Los Tontos.

Two cautions. Awwwards technology tags are self-reported and three contradict the live site: Tripletta is tagged Contentful but is served from Webflow, and Ballena is tagged Contentful but declares WordPress and Elementor. Check the page source yourself. And 2023 is thinly covered, so this is a picture of 2024 to 2026.

## Two curves and a sticky container build most signature effects

The densest free source of per-effect recipes with exact numbers is Olivier Larose's tutorial site: about 75 tutorials, each with an article, a video, a repository and a live demo ([Olivier Larose](https://blog.olivierlarose.com/tutorials)). His public tutorials stop in mid-2024 and use older package names, so the values transfer but imports need updating. GSAP's own Helper Functions page gives the canonical code for marquees, draggable loops and image sequences ([GSAP](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/HelperFunctions/)). Codrops is the most current source, with 2026 tutorials on kinetic type, infinite galleries and page transitions; those are listed below by title and date only, because the article bodies could not be read.

|---|---|---|

One structure repeats across the scroll effects: **a tall container, a sticky 100vh child, and scroll progress mapped to a value**. Zoom parallax, stacking cards and horizontal scroll are all that one pattern, so an agent can treat it as a single reusable part. Larose's own horizontal-scroll tutorial is behind his paid course ([Olivier Larose](https://blog.olivierlarose.com/tutorials/horizontal-section)). Three effects have thin tutorial coverage: preloaders, text scramble and number counters. Learn those from the full landing-page tutorial and the official plugin pages ([ScrambleText docs](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/Plugins/ScrambleTextPlugin/)).

Older advice that the Next.js App Router cannot do page transitions is out of date. The official guide, updated 18 November 2026, says view transitions work with no configuration, with one limit: the browser's back and forward buttons do not animate ([Next.js](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/guides/view-transitions)).

### The easing set is small and exact

The easings.net values are the shared vocabulary. These six cover a whole site; the "use" column reflects what the award-style tutorials do, which is a strong convention, not a measured survey of winners.

| Token | Value | Use | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ease-out cubic | `cubic-bezier(0.33, 1, 0.68, 1)` | Text and element reveals | 0.6 to 0.8 s |
| Ease-out quart | `cubic-bezier(0.25, 1, 0.5, 1)` | Snappier reveals, hovers | shorter |
| Ease-out expo | `cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)` | Snappier reveals, hovers | shorter |
| Ease-in-out quart | `cubic-bezier(0.76, 0, 0.24, 1)` | Preloader exit, full-screen wipes, page transitions | 0.7 to 1.0 s |
| Ease-in cubic | `cubic-bezier(0.32, 0, 0.67, 0)` | Exits only | about 0.4 s |
| Linear (`ease: "none"`) | none | Anything looping or tied to scroll | n/a |

Source for the values: [easings.net source file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ai/easings.net/master/src/easings.yml). Elastic and bounce curves have no cubic-bezier form, so those need GSAP or a spring. GSAP's own helpers set linear ease for loops and scrubbed sequences ([GSAP](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/HelperFunctions/helpers/seamlessLoop)).

### The performance budget is three numbers

Google's "good" thresholds are **LCP within 2.5 seconds, INP of 200 ms or less, and CLS of 0.1 or less**, measured at the 75th percentile of visits ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/vitals)). LCP is how long the largest visible element takes to appear, INP is how quickly the page responds to a tap, and CLS is how much the layout jumps. The rules that protect them are short. Animate only `transform` and `opacity`. Use `will-change` rarely, add it just before an animation and remove it after ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/animations-guide)). Put `fetchpriority="high"` on the hero image; in a Google Flights test that alone moved LCP from 2.6 to 1.9 seconds. Lazy-load everything below the fold, and mark off-screen carousel slides low priority ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/fetch-priority)). Serve fonts as WOFF2, choose `font-display` deliberately, and use `size-adjust` on the fallback font so text does not jump when the real font arrives ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/font-best-practices)). Turn marquees off entirely for visitors who ask for reduced motion ([Ryan Mulligan](https://ryanmulligan.dev/blog/css-marquee/)).

The preloader is the main trap. Using the tutorial's own timings, a word cycle of 1.0 s plus seven words at 0.15 s plus a 0.8 s exit is about 2.9 seconds. If the hero only paints after that, the page has already missed the 2.5 second budget. This is arithmetic from the recipe, not a field measurement, but the fix is simple: let the hero image load and paint underneath the loader, and keep the loader short. A similar caution applies to image sequences: the reference examples use 147 or 148 frames, and the CSS-Tricks article notes Apple serves a single still on slow connections ([CSS-Tricks](https://css-tricks.com/lets-make-one-of-those-fancy-scrolling-animations-used-on-apple-product-pages/)).

### What to pay for, and what to start from

Everything in the table above is free. If you pay for one thing, the three price bands are clear. Copy-paste effect libraries cost about €20 to €25 a month ([Osmo](https://www.osmo.supply/plans), [Made With GSAP](https://madewithgsap.com/)). Deep one-time courses run $249 to $599: Emil Kowalski's [animations.dev](https://animations.dev/) at $299 is the one about judgment (what good motion is), and Josh Comeau's [Whimsical Animations](https://whimsy.joshwcomeau.com/) at $249 to $389 covers SVG, canvas and cursor work. [Three.js Journey](https://threejs-journey.com/) is $95 if you go into 3D. For a first restaurant build, none is required.

For starting code, licence decides what you can use. **MIT-licensed starters you can build on**: darkroom's [satus](https://github.com/darkroomengineering/satus) (Next.js 16, Tailwind 4, Lenis, Sanity integration, agent instructions and asset-weight budgets included; it uses anime.js, not GSAP), Codrops' [Interlude](https://github.com/codrops/Interlude) (Astro 7, GSAP, Lenis, Three.js), and the single-effect Codrops demo repositories such as [MagneticButtons](https://github.com/codrops/MagneticButtons). **Study-only repositories with no licence file** include Olivier Larose's [landing page](https://github.com/olivierlarose/awwwards-landing-page), basement.studio's [2025 site](https://github.com/basementstudio/website-2k25) and Adrian Hajdin's clones such as [gsap_cocktails](https://github.com/adrianhajdin/gsap_cocktails); no licence means all rights reserved, so read them for structure and values and do not copy code into client work. For Claude Code specifically, install the official [GSAP skills](https://github.com/greensock/gsap-skills), which teach agents correct GSAP usage, and Vercel's view-transitions skill named in the [Next.js guide](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/guides/view-transitions).

## Small senior teams skip the handoff and ration their motion

The 2025 to 2026 Codrops studio spotlights, written by the studios themselves, describe nearly the same shop each time: a compact core of senior people who cross design, motion and code, with freelancers added per project. Exo Ape is three partners and says "a vision should never be handed off or diluted as it moves through the studio" ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/02/09/exo-ape-crafting-unforgettable-digital-journeys/)). Obys says "Designers understand development. Developers understand motion", and insists on fundamentals first: "You can break the grid only if you understand it" ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/03/06/obys-the-small-studio-designing-big-digital-narratives/)). Novelty comes from standing internal experiments, weekly at Basement and monthly at Lusion, which reach clients only after they work ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/12/15/from-basement-to-breakthroughs-inside-the-studio-powering-the-internets-boldest-brands/), [Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/04/13/lusion-where-digital-craft-meets-ambitious-experimentation/)).

The Awwwards budget figures are fields studios fill in themselves and were read from search summaries, so treat them as minimum job size, not typical price. Simple arithmetic on the published day rates puts a two or three person, four to six week studio build at roughly $50,000 to $65,000, which matches the directory bands. The practical reading for a solo builder is that the gap between a $10,000 freelancer and a $50,000 studio is mostly head count, not day rate.

Zajno's public partner guide is the clearest process template to borrow. A kickoff call fixes expectations. Design direction takes **7 to 14 days**, starting with a proof of concept that locks the art direction. One or two directions are presented, "quality over quantity". **Two revision rounds** are included and further rounds are billed. The client gives consolidated feedback **within 48 hours**. Photography stays as placeholder until the client supplies it, and the client buys the fonts ([Zajno](https://zajno-process.web.app)). The levers studios publish are commercial ones (directions, rounds, feedback deadline, who supplies fonts and photos), and that is where projects slip or hold.

In-house teams say the same thing from the other side. Linear rejects workshop process outright ("no infinite-loop processes, workshops, or sticky notes were involved") and pairs engineers with designers every afternoon ([Linear](https://linear.app/now/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui)). Vercel's design engineers hold "no dropped frames, no cross-browser inconsistencies, and accessibility" as the bar ([Vercel](https://vercel.com/blog/design-engineering-at-vercel)). Stripe's head of design describes sketching, having an AI tool assemble the sketch from real components, then pushing on it by hand: "Prototyping, not presenting", with the warning that AI "doesn't replace craft, it doesn't replace taste" ([YC Library](https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Nz-how-stripe-built-their-new-website)). That is the workflow a designer with Claude Code already has.

Two rules on how much motion to use carry straight over to a restaurant site. Rauno Freiberg: frequent interactions become a "cognitive burden after seeing the same animation for the hundredth time", so menus and repeated actions should be near-instant ([rauno.me](https://rauno.me/craft/interaction-design)). Benji Taylor: "Mastering delight is mastering selective emphasis", meaning more delight on rare moments and less on frequent ones ([benji.org](https://benji.org/family-values)). Spend motion on the loader, the hero and the 404 page; keep the navigation, the menu tabs and the reserve button fast. Monzo adds a counterweight to hero obsession: on its redesigned page, below-the-fold content drove more of the 9.7% conversion gain than the hero did ([Monzo](https://monzo.com/blog/reimagining-monzo-com-building-a-modular-high-fidelity-web-design-system-that-scales)).

Rive and Lottie are not part of the award-studio pipeline; none of the studio accounts mentions either. They appear in software marketing teams where a motion designer makes a self-contained illustration and an engineer embeds it. The one quantified case, Intercom halving file size by moving from Lottie to Rive, is published by Rive itself ([Rive](https://rive.app/blog/intercom-s-product-animation-evolution-embracing-rive)). For a code-built restaurant site, skip both unless you need one bounded animated illustration.

| Letter spacing | [Inter dynamic metrics](https://d.rsms.me/inter-website/v3/dynmetrics/) | None for body text; about -1.5% to -2.5% for display sizes (computed from Inter's formula) |
| Grid | [IBM Carbon](https://carbondesignsystem.com/elements/2x-grid/overview/) | 4 columns on phones, 8 on tablets, 16 on desktop; 32 px gutter; everything on an 8 px unit |
| Colour roles | [Radix Colors](https://www.radix-ui.com/colors/docs/palette-composition/understanding-the-scale) | 12 steps per colour: 1 to 2 backgrounds, 3 to 5 component fills, 6 to 8 borders, 9 the solid brand colour, 11 to 12 text |
| Colour ramps | [Refactoring UI](https://www.refactoringui.com/previews/building-your-color-palette) | 8 to 10 greys; pick base, darkest and lightest by hand, then fill between; never generate shades by formula |
| Contrast | [Stripe](https://stripe.com/blog/accessible-color-systems) | Same lightness curve for every hue; five steps apart is safe for small text (4.5:1) |

Utopia's reason for two ratios is that a larger screen can carry more dramatic size contrast; its worked example goes as far as 1.333 at 1500 px ([Utopia](https://utopia.fyi/blog/designing-with-fluid-type-scales)). For a restaurant, that means the big serif headline grows faster than the body text as the screen widens. Applied to the palette pattern above: take the two brand colours, put the deep one at step 9 of its own ramp and the warm light at steps 1 to 2 of a tinted neutral, and build the remaining steps by hand. The 60-30-10 colour rule has no authoritative source and is best treated as folklore.

## Restaurants are quoted hundreds, so a $10K fee must be built

Among 20 small studios that publish prices, checked 16 to 17 November 2026, the median starting price for a full site is **$12,000**, and "from" is a floor: Goodspeed starts at $10,000 but says most first builds land at $15,000 to $25,000; INSAIM starts at $8,000 but quotes about $15,000 for 20 pages. Studios under $25,000 quote 3 to 12 weeks. Fourteen of the 20 build on Webflow and four write custom code ([DIXI](https://www.dixi.agency/blog/saas-web-design-agencies-pricing)). All of this comes through one competitor-compiled comparison, and the buyers are software and business-to-business companies.

The restaurant market is priced very differently. Public restaurant offers start at $850 one-time or $99 to $199 a month ([Bripe Media](https://design.bripemedia.com/web-design/restaurants), [Webdiner](https://webdiner.com/pricing/)), and the specialist platforms charge $179 to $499 a month ([Popmenu](https://get.popmenu.com/pricing)). No restaurant-focused studio publishing a $10,000 price was found, and no solo freelancer's first-hand pricing account either. So a $10,000 restaurant quote is an extrapolation from another market, and it competes against a very low anchor. An earlier pass of this research found the same thing from the other direction: a hospitality agency's own tiers put $10,000+ at multi-location architecture, gift cards, event booking and custom reservation integration or a full brand identity, not at a five-page animated site ([Blue Ridge](https://weareblueridge.com/how-much-does-a-restaurant-website-cost/)). One vendor does price custom motion as a line item: complex GSAP scroll sequences or WebGL "add $3,000 to $10,000" ([Flowmarc](https://flowmarc.com/blog/how-much-does-a-webflow-agency-cost-in-2026)). That is a search excerpt from a vendor, so treat it as a lead.

The proposal templates agree on a shape. Four phases: discovery, design, build, then test, launch and train. A stated number of page templates, not just pages. Numbered revision rounds with sign-off before build. An hourly rate for anything outside scope ($125 in two templates). A list of exclusions. Staged payment: **50% on acceptance, 25% on design approval, 25% at launch** ([Nusii](https://nusii.com/proposal-templates/web-design-proposal-template/), [Clientary](https://www.clientary.com/templates/web-design/website-design-proposal)). The Nusii example also includes a recorded 45-minute training session, 30 days of fixes after launch, redirects from every old address, and a care plan named with its price. These are templates with illustrative numbers; no real signed proposal at this level was found.

Three tactics transfer well to a solo builder. The first is the **credited audit**: charge a small fee for a review ($400 at Foursets, $1,500 at DIXI) and deduct it from the build, which turns a free pitch into paid discovery ([DIXI](https://www.dixi.agency/blog/saas-web-design-agencies-pricing)). The second is **tying the fee to one number**: the Nusii example reasons that ten more bookings a month is about $4,000, sets a baseline, and schedules a review 60 days after launch. Do this in place of promising an uplift, because the only restaurant case study with figures is weak: it is agency-written, covers four marketing channels at once, compares 2020 with 2019, and its headline "214% increase" from 1,400 to 3,000 visits is really 114% ([Big Storm](https://greatbigstorm.com/feast-restaurant-marketing-success-case-study/)). The third is **written answers to buyer fears**: the client owns the domain, hosting, code and accounts; one named person does the work; photography and copy are priced or assigned; scope changes have a stated rate ([DIXI](https://www.dixi.agency/blog/saas-web-design-agencies-pricing)). The objection a solo builder cannot argue away is being a single point of failure, so answer it with client-owned accounts and a documented handover.

For recurring income, care plans with a real person attached cluster at **$150 to $350 a month** for small-business sites; automated-only plans run $50 to $150; and one vendor puts restaurant maintenance at $59 to $250 because "menus, hours and reservations must always work" ([GruffyGoat](https://gruffygoat.com/blog/website-maintenance-cost), [WP Website Maintenance Packages](https://wpwebsitemaintenancepackages.com/website-maintenance-cost/)). The contract should list tasks, monthly hours, the overage rate, response times and backups ([Up Business Co](https://upbusinessco.com/blogs/website-maintenance-cost/)). Design subscriptions at $3,000 to $6,000 a month suit clients with constant design work, which a single restaurant does not have.

A rough five-year comparison, using simple arithmetic on these prices: Popmenu's middle plan is about $17,900 before order fees; a $12,000 custom build plus $85 a month of stack plus a $200 care plan is about $29,100. The custom site is in the same range as the top platform plan but does not include the ordering, loyalty and email tools the platforms bundle. Say that plainly to the client, and show the reservation and ordering fees as pass-through costs that exist whoever builds the site.

Reservations go onto a custom site by pasting the vendor's script or linking out, not through a programming interface. OpenTable's route is: dashboard, Widgets, Booking, choose embedded or floating button, select "Restaurant website", copy the script into the page where it should appear ([OpenTable support](https://support.opentable.com/s/article/How-do-I-install-the-reservation-widget-to-take-reservations-on-my-website-and-Facebook)). Because these are third-party scripts, they are the main threat to speed and visual consistency on a tuned site. The mitigation recommended here, as judgment not sourced fact, is a styled button that links to the booking page, or a widget loaded only when the visitor opens the booking panel. That matches what the award sites do: Clos des Sens links to hosted Zenchef pages and Ballena links to OpenTable.

For search, Google documents the structured data. Use `@type: Restaurant` with `name` and `address` (required), then `geo` (latitude and longitude to at least five decimal places), `telephone`, `url`, `menu` (the full address of the menu page), `servesCuisine`, `priceRange` (under 100 characters), `openingHoursSpecification`, and images in 16x9, 4x3 and 1x1. **Leave out your own star rating**: Google says review markup is for sites that collect reviews about other businesses, so a restaurant marking up its own stars should not expect them to show ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business)). Because `menu` takes a web address, the menu needs a stable, crawlable HTML page, which is also what Ballena, Los Tontos and Onyx do. The earlier research pass adds that the name, address and hours should match the Google Business Profile ([Beyond Menu](https://get.beyondmenu.com/blog/restaurant-menu-seo)); Google's own profile requirements were not sourced in this round.

## A ten-step restaurant build with Claude Code

This sequence joins the four areas into one order of work. The stack choice is a recommendation: Next.js on the satus starter if the owner will edit menus in Sanity, or the Interlude Astro starter if you want GSAP page transitions pre-wired and less machinery. Both are MIT-licensed and maintained as of November 2026.

| Step | Do this | Detail from the research |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Collect six references | Three restaurant sites from the Awwwards Honorable Mention tier, one from Httpster, one footer, one navigation. Note the Element names you want |
| 2 | Decide the hero as a choice | Two actions: reserve and view menu. Keep reserve in the header on every page |
| 3 | Fix the visual system | One display face plus one text face; two brand colours (deep ground, warm light); Utopia scale 1.2 to 1.25; 8 px spacing unit; 4 and 16 column grid |
| 4 | Write the motion vocabulary | Four items at most: masked line reveal, one marquee, one hover-image list, one page transition. Name the two easing curves and durations |
| 5 | Put steps 2 to 4 in a design file for the agent | Tokens as exact values; state what must not animate (navigation, menu tabs, reserve button) |
| 6 | Start from an MIT starter and install the GSAP skills | Do not copy from unlicensed repositories |
| 7 | Build the menu as HTML first | Sections, dish name, one-line ingredients, dietary flags, price or deliberate omission; editable in the CMS |
| 8 | Add booking as a link or a late-loaded widget | Use the restaurant's existing platform; never build reservations |
| 9 | Add the edges | Short loader with the hero painting underneath, page transition, designed footer and 404 page |
| 10 | Check the three numbers and ship the data | LCP 2.5 s, INP 200 ms, CLS 0.1 on a phone; reduced-motion fallbacks; Restaurant structured data without self-authored ratings |

## Conclusion

The deeper sources change the picture in one specific way: the gap between an ordinary restaurant site and an award-recognised one is narrower and cheaper than the gap between either and a studio showcase. Honorable Mention restaurants run on Webflow or WordPress with three small libraries, and what separates them is restraint in type and colour plus a handful of well-made edge details. That is within reach of one designer working with Claude Code, and it does not need WebGL, video generation or a paid course.

The hard part has moved to the commercial side. The technique is documented to the decimal, but nobody publishes a restaurant paying $10,000 for a site, a freelancer explaining how they charged it, or a clean measurement of what a redesign did for bookings. A realistic path is to build the first site as a portfolio piece at whatever the client will pay, agree one booking metric and a baseline before starting, and measure it for 60 days. That measured result would be evidence this research could not find anywhere, and it is the strongest thing to put in front of the second client. Before quoting any figure from this report to a client, re-check it at its source: price pages changed in five of five spot checks made by one of the sources used here.
